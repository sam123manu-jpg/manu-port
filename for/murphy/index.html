Hi Murphy team, I've built you a stop in my city. Your posting says onboarding a new bank still takes weeks of manual configuration by engineers, and asks for evals that prove a change is real before it ships to a bank. I've done the first job before: at XY ADS I built a self-service ad exchange from scratch and moved clients off campaigns we ran for them by hand, with $100k a month in ad spend running through it by the time I left. Below is how I'd turn a bank's setup into product, and the gate I'd put in front of every change.

  1. What I looked at

    Your posting; your website's product, compliance and market pages, read on 29 October 2026; your Forward Deployed Engineer and Product Data Analyst postings; and Seedcamp's and Northzone's notes on your 2025 round. I haven't seen the platform or sat in on a deployment, so this is an outside read and a plan to test, not a verdict.

  2. Where the weeks go: the same five decisions for every bank

    Put your website's pages side by side and each new bank brings the same set of decisions. How its data arrives: a file upload, a schedule, or a live dialer and CRM link. The rules of each market it collects in: each of your 14 markets sets its own call openings, calling hours and recording rules. The negotiation limits for each product, such as a discount of up to 20% or a first payment within 14 days. The contact plan, such as voice calls at least four hours apart and two a day at most. And a compliance manager's approval, because only they can approve a change. Most of these repeat from bank to bank, and only the values change. Where engineers enter those values by hand, each bank costs engineering time and each later change a ticket. Some of the weeks may also go on the bank's side, in security reviews and data access, and some of this may already be templated; that's what the first step finds out. Your Forward Deployed Engineer posting already asks for configurations that are "maintainable and scalable across clients" and no "one-off solutions". This role is where that becomes product.

  3. What I'd build first

    Time it before building anything. On the next two or three banks I'd sit with the deployment engineers and log every configuration step: how long it takes, who waits on whom, and which values had to change after go-live. That log separates the waits that are ours from the ones on the bank's side, and decides which of the five gets built first. Before engineers build it, I'd prototype the setup with Claude Code against a sample bank file, so the first version is tested on real data. Then turn what repeats into defaults. A rule pack for each market, kept up to date once with Compliance and reused by every bank in that market. Negotiation limits and the contact plan as a form with starting values for each debt stage, not code, with a model drafting them from the bank's own collections policy for its compliance manager to check line by line. And a conflict check before launch: a contact plan that would call outside a market's calling hours, or two rules that contradict each other, gets flagged before a customer's phone rings. Then hand the values to the bank: its compliance manager sets and approves them in the product, on the version history and approval trail you already keep, with a deployment engineer reviewing the first ones. Engineers keep the data connection, the part that needs them. What I'd measure: days from signed contract to first live call, engineer hours per bank, changes after go-live that still need an engineer, and setup errors caught before launch versus after.

  4. One gate for every change

    Your agents improve through experiments on segments, voice, cadence and script. Every such change, and every new bank's setup, would pass the same gate before a bank hears it. First offline: run a fixed set of simulated conversations for each market and debt stage, with the customer side played by a model or a person, including the hard ones, such as a customer who disputes the debt, a vulnerable customer, a wrong number, or someone who asks for a person. Score them with the compliance checks that already score every live call, plus a check that the agent stayed inside the bank's limits. A change that fails a compliance case doesn't ship, whatever it does to recovery. Every failed call your human reviewers pick up becomes a new test case, so the set grows from real failures. Then live: a share of accounts stays on the current version, and the change is measured on what the bank pays for, the share of balance collected, alongside promises to pay (read early) and promises kept (read when they fall due), and cost per euro collected, with complaints and escalations as guardrails. Your data analyst posting talks about telling "a real lift in recovery from noise"; I'd agree the sample size with that team before a test starts, not after. The failure modes I'd watch: an agent that agrees a plan the customer can't afford, which only shows up weeks later as broken promises, and a script that scores well in replay and collects less on the phone.

  5. I've turned a service into a product before

    XY ADS was a high-volume performance marketing agency, and I joined with no ad-tech background. Small businesses found agency platforms too complex, so we ran their campaigns for them by hand. I built a self-service ad exchange from scratch and moved clients onto it: $100k a month in ad spend ran through it by the time I left, and one client grew to $30k a month. I wrote the API-level PRDs, architected it for 100M+ monthly impressions, and integrated many demand and supply partners by breaking each partner's API docs into the exact APIs our developers had to build. I designed role-based access so agency teams could run campaigns under central master accounts, routed back-end services such as billing between providers by traffic, cost and stability, and cut operating costs 15% by moving vendors to tiered, volume-based contracts. I hired three developers and a designer, who reported to me. Today, at MR Neo, a CRM and automation company, I designed flow-conflict detection for its automation builder, so users see which flows overlap, contradict or supersede each other before they break: the same check I'd want on a bank's contact plan.

  6. Where it could go in 12 months

    A lender sets itself up, starting with the mid-market clients your sales team is now hiring for. It picks its markets and gets each market's rule pack, sends a sample file, sets its limits and contact plan from starting values, watches its setup pass the test gate, and its compliance manager approves the first live call. Adding a market means writing its rule pack once, not reconfiguring every bank. Deployment engineers spend their time on new integrations and new markets instead of re-entering values, and when they hit something the product can't do, it comes back as a product request, which your FDE posting already asks them to write. The same gate then covers every experiment, so a better script or cadence reaches every bank it suits, not only the one where it was found.

  7. Why me, why Murphy

    I've built from zero and scaled: the ad exchange from nothing to $100k a month in ad spend, and GMNG, a real-money gaming app, to 1M+ users and $500k+ in deposits, leading a team of nine across development, support and sales. Money and rules aren't new to me: at GMNG I led our KYC verification flow, from implementation to testing, and cut what we paid our verification provider by reusing data across its separately billed API calls. I ran A/B tests and feature flags in Firebase. And the biggest lesson of my career came from a deposit drop-off: I'd launched card payments because my friends and I paid by card, talked to the players who did deposit, found 80% used Paytm, and moved the gateway to it. In collections, that's reaching people on the channel and at the hour they actually answer, which your agents already learn. I'm AI-native: at MR Neo I made the whole company AI-native in under two months, audited all 60+ pages of its platform, and turned my own testing method into reusable AI skills the team now uses. I built this city with Claude Code, and on Lará, the AI hotel app I'm prototyping, allergy alerts never depend on the AI. I haven't run evals on voice agents yet; the gate above is how I'd start. I'm based in Lisbon, ready to relocate to Dubai, and available full-time from mid-December 2026.
