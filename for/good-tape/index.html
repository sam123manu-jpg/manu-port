Hi Good Tape team, I've built you a stop in my city. Your role owns the path from signup to paid, so I read it the way a reporter meets it: one interview to transcribe today, the next one next week.

  1. The flow I looked at

    Your public conversion path on 24 October 2026: the homepage, the pricing page and its FAQ, the main FAQ, the free-trial page, the journalism page, signup up to the first form, the Business page and the help centre. I compared the free and paid plans with Happy Scribe and Otter. I kept to what any visitor can check, seen from Lisbon (prices may differ by country), so anything in-product below is a question, not a claim.

  2. What I noticed

    Your free offer is generous: an hour of Pro transcription with no card, then a free plan of three 30-minute transcripts a month. But no single page tells a visitor that journey. The pricing card says "1 hour of transcription" with no number of days. The FAQ under it says "3-day Free Trial". The main FAQ describes the free plan, and its "See our free plan details" link opens a page about the one-hour trial that never mentions days. So a reporter deciding whether to start has to piece the clock, and the free plan after it, together from three places. The path is long too: the homepage "Start free trial" and blog "Sign up" buttons go to the pricing page first, while the journalism page goes straight to signup, and signup then asks "What industry do you work in?".

  3. What I'd ship, then test

    First, the cheap part: make the pricing card, both FAQs and the free page say the same thing, "Try Pro free for 3 days with an hour of transcription, no card." Whether to add "then keep a free plan of three 30-minute transcripts a month" is your pricing call, so I'd bring it as a split test, not a before-and-after: October also brought Explore, your funding news and a new term. Then test one surface at a time. The trial-end moment: show what someone used, what they keep and what they'd lose, with the "Try for €10" offer in view, against today's screen. The homepage, with your Growth Marketing Manager: "Start free trial" straight to signup against going through pricing, with paid-first signups as a guardrail. The pricing page: "Save 20%" instead of "Extra savings" (€192 against €240 a year; Happy Scribe and Otter print theirs), and the "Try for €10" offer visible in the default yearly view, not only under Monthly. Primary metric: paid revenue per signup at day 30, read again at day 90 for people on the free plan, with checkout completion alongside. Guardrails: yearly mix, free transcription cost per signup, and refunds.

  4. My first question, and the learning agenda behind it

    The first thing I'd pull with your data scientist is where trials end today: before the first tape, after one tape, at the one-hour cap, or at the three-day clock? Reporters and researchers often have one interview this week and the next one later, so the answer decides whether the lever is clarity, the trial-end moment, or the trial itself. It opens a short pipeline of hypotheses, each with its own test: does a clear journey lift trial starts; does a designed trial-end moment lift conversion; is the clock or the hour the real limit; do the three signup questions earn their place; and does "With a team" at signup predict Business demand? With Product and Engineering I'd turn each launch into a test, starting with Explore: does using it during the trial predict Pro conversion now, and add-on uptake once the launch offer ends?

  5. Where it could go in 12 months

    Transcription on its own is becoming free on phones and in meeting tools, and Good Tape is already moving past it with Explore. I'd propose defining activation as a second tape plus one thing a phone can't do (speaker labels, summaries or Explore), not just a first transcript, and moving the upgrade ask to that value, because an archive gets more useful with every tape. The next step is from one reporter to a whole desk: signup already asks "Just me / With a team", while Business today is "Contact sales" only, so turning that answer into leads for Sales, and testing self-serve seats for small teams with them, is worth doing. Along the way, funnel and experiment results would feed your pricing and positioning work, read in trial-to-paid, paid conversions per signup, yearly mix and add-on uptake.

  6. Why it matters for the role

    At MR Neo, a CRM and automation company, I reset pricing and positioning with competitor benchmarking and try-before-you-upgrade add-ons, made the company AI-native in under two months, and turned my own testing method into reusable AI skills the whole team now uses. Before that, at GMNG, I cut sign-up drop-off by about 60% by replacing the registration form with a single phone-number field and collecting profile details later, removed wait times by integrating third-party services (+20% revenue), and used Firebase Remote Config for feature flags and A/B tests. I diagnosed loss aversion behind new-user churn and designed Everybody Wins, which lifted new-user retention 30%. I led a team of nine there.
