Hi Zalando team, I've built you a stop in my city. Most Inventory work is invisible until a customer tells you their size. I filtered women's sneakers to size 39 and checked the first 48: for 1 in 5 of the pairs in stock, size 39 cost more than the tile said, every time sold by a partner, and usually with a later delivery window. Below is how I'd measure it, what I'd test and where it leads. I'm open to relocating to Berlin and available from mid-December.

  1. The flow I looked at

    On en.zalando.de I filtered women's sneakers to size 39 and opened the first 48 products on desktop, logged out, on 24 October 2026, then rechecked the listing and a product page in a 375px layout. For each I compared the listing tile with size 39's price, seller and delivery window, and added two to the bag to see how it splits. As lighter controls I checked the first 24 men's jackets in size M, and the 12 "From" tiles for size 6 on zalando.co.uk. Rankings are personalised, so this is one session, not a sample of the shop.

  2. What I noticed

    A shopper filters to size 39 and sees adidas Samba OG "From €107.95, up to -10%". In the product's own data, sizes up to 38⅔ are Zalando's stock at €107.95, arriving Tuesday; every in-stock size from 39⅓ to 46⅔ is adidas's own stock at €119.95, arriving Tuesday to Thursday. The discount on the tile doesn't exist in size 39, and filtered to size 41 the tile reads the same. The filter mostly works: 45 of the 48 had size 39 in stock, and of the three misses, two are adidas sizing in thirds and one was sold out. But one product's sizes are sold by different sellers, each with its own price and delivery window, and the tile summarises the product, not the size the customer asked for. In 9 of 45, size 39 cost more than the tile (median +25%, up to +133%), all sold by partners, and in 8 of those 9 the delivery window ended later than for Zalando's own stock. No tile showed a seller or a delivery date. The first 24 men's jackets in size M had no gap, which suggests it concentrates where brands fill part of a size run.

  3. What I'd check, then test

    As I understand it, Search owns the tile, Pricing and partners set prices, and the delivery promise is predicted across the fulfilment network; underneath all three sits what Inventory owns: which stock, in which location, backs each size. The tile could show the cheapest size by design, because price is aggregated per product, because of caching, or because of size mapping. Each leaves a different trace: aggregation gives the same tile on every size filter (size 41 already does), caching gives gaps that close within minutes, and mapping appears only on brands that size in thirds. So first, with data science and engineering, I'd measure how often a size-filtered tile's price, and separately its delivery window, differs from that size's best offer, by seller type and category, and what happens to exits, size switching and bag abandonment when it does. The UK product page already flags single sizes as "Long-distance item", with a later window and a fee, so a size-level promise already reaches the product page; I'd ask what it would take for the listing to read it too. If the gap costs orders, I'd test in steps, each a user-split A/B test, so every result reads cleanly: price the tile from the filtered size, then add a delivery hint, then, with Search, a "fast in my size" signal. Primary metric: orders per size-filtered session. Guardrails: partner GMV share, returns, stock-related cancellations and search latency. Partner sizes will look pricier and slower because for that shopper they are, so Commercial agrees the trade-off before anything ships.

  4. The KPIs I'd own

    Promise accuracy at size level, measured separately for price and delivery: what the listing says against what reaches the bag. Oversell and stock-related cancellations by stock pool, kept apart from other cancellations. Time from a stock event to that stock being sellable everywhere. I'd baseline all three in the first month, set the targets from that baseline with the team, and rank every experiment by which one it moves. The goal they serve: a size-filtered listing that is never wrong about price or delivery, starting with sneakers and then every category where a size run is split across stock pools.

  5. Where it could go in 12 months

    Inventory serves stock from many owners to many front ends: Zalando's own stock, partners' stock in your warehouses, partners shipping themselves, and ZEOS merchants selling on other channels. Working backwards from a customer who is never surprised in the bag, the direction is one size-level answer to "can we sell this here, at what price, arriving when" that every surface reads, including what a stock count misses: units already reserved, and whether a unit may be sold in that channel. I'd start by reusing what exists: the size-level offer the product page already shows, and the UK's per-size delivery label. I'd defer new work in the stock pipeline, such as fresher partner updates, until cancellations by stock pool show where stale stock costs orders. A new shared capability earns its build only if the listing can't get a correct size-level price and promise from existing services at acceptable speed.

  6. Why it matters for the role

    At XY ADS I owned the build of a self-serve ad exchange, a two-sided marketplace, from scratch: I wrote the API-level OpenRTB PRDs, architected it for 100M+ monthly impressions, and turned each DSP's and SSP's API docs into the exact APIs our developers built. I hired and led three developers and a designer, and by the time I left $100k a month in ad spend ran through it. At GMNG I led a team of nine and grew the platform to 1M+ users. When new players quit after losing their first game, I diagnosed loss aversion rather than treating the drop-off, and gave them a second chance: new-user retention rose 30%. At MR Neo I made the company AI-native in under two months and turned my testing method into AI skills the whole team now uses. I'm now prototyping Lará, an AI hotel operations platform, with AI coding tools, and I'd prototype the size-aware tile the same way.
