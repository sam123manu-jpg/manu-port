Hi Dalux team, I've built you a stop in my city. This year Dalux shipped two AI features, alongside AI translation, and both get the hardest call right: a person decides. The AI Assistant answers only from the open document and cites PDFs; AI Safety hints never raise an issue on their own. The question I'd want answered first is how anyone knows Safety hints is good: how often it's right, and what it misses. Below is how I'd measure that, what "good" would mean for both features, and where AI isn't the answer.

  1. What I looked at

    On 24 October 2026 I read Dalux's help-centre articles for the AI Assistant in Box Pro and AI Safety hints in Field Pro, and the Build release notes from 2025.8 to 2026.6: the Assistant rolled out in phases from February and reached every Box Pro project in June, and Safety hints arrived in August. I compared both with what Procore, Autodesk and Thinkproject have shipped. I haven't used either feature on a live project, so this is the documented design, not observed use. The first thing I'd do in the role is sit with a safety manager working through a week of hints, and with a site team asking the Assistant real questions.

  2. What I noticed

    A SiteWalk is recorded, the AI flags a scene and explains why, and the safety manager reviews it. They can create a safety issue, pre-filled with a title, description and cropped screenshot, or, per the release note, close the hint. With no action it's removed after 30 days, opened or not. That's a sensible v1: nothing is filed without a person, and stale hints don't pile up. It also means the product gets one clear signal, an issue created. A closed or expired hint could be a false alarm, a hazard already handled, or a busy week, and nothing in the documentation says the site's reason is captured. Some of that may be deliberate, because a logged "not a hazard" is evidence after an incident. Dalux has reported more than a million SiteWalk 360° images a month, so as Safety hints spreads, telling these cases apart decides where the model needs work. The AI Assistant faces a quieter version of the same question. Every answer has thumbs up or down, with a comment on thumbs down. But it reads one text document at a time, partly by design, so a question whose answer sits on a drawing or in another document should end in "I can't find that", which is correct, but in the feedback it looks the same as a miss. And documents over 100 pages aren't supported yet, while a full specification often runs longer, so some of the questions that most need an assistant never reach it.

  3. What I'd check, then test

    I'd start where users do no extra work. First, what the product already logs: opened or not, closed or expired. Second, precision from the images themselves: each week a reviewer labels a sample of hints as a real hazard or not, by hazard type. SiteWalk footage shows identifiable workers, so that sample comes from customers who agree to it in their contract. Dalux may already do this, and if so I'd start from those labels. Third, recall, because precision alone is dangerous for safety: a model that flags less looks better. By default, safety managers only see walks where a hint was raised, so recall needs consented walks plus staged walks with planted hazards: a reviewer marks every hazard they see, and we count how many the AI found. Only if that shows "already handled" is a large share of closed hints would I test an optional reason on Close, with legal agreeing the wording first. Guardrails throughout: hints per SiteWalk, to watch alert fatigue, and time from hint to issue. For the Assistant, I'd tag each thumbs-down by question type, so that "answer is on a drawing" stops being counted as a wrong answer.

  4. What "good" would mean

    For Safety hints: precision per hazard type from the reviewed sample, always reported next to recall from the sampled walks, and the hint-to-issue rate per project, read as usage rather than precision, because dismissing a flagged hazard leaves a record some managers will avoid. For the AI Assistant: a test set built from real Dalux questions, sorted by where the answer lives (a spec clause, a table, a drawing, another document, a file over the 100-page limit, an older version, or nowhere). Each answer gets three scores. Faithful: every claim is backed by the document. Cited correctly, for PDFs until .docx gets citations: the citation supports the sentence, it doesn't just exist. Refused correctly: it says "I can't find that" exactly when the answer isn't there. Benchmarks such as GaRAGe (ACL 2025) show models often answer when they should say they can't, so refusals get their own score. Every thumbs-down comment becomes a candidate test case, and cost and latency per question type sit next to quality. Targets come from a first-month baseline, set with the team. No change ships if it improves one score by hurting another.

  5. Where it could go in 12 months

    The Assistant already answers in the user's language, whatever the document's language. On a European site with a Danish spec and a Polish crew, that matters, and Thinkproject's project-wide assistant works in English and German only and doesn't search across languages. Across a whole project it needs retrieval that works across languages, so it's an edge to protect. The direction is from one document to one project, with a citation for every claim and the same tests gating each step. Some questions shouldn't go to a model at all. "Which revision is current?" and "Where is room 2.14?" are lookups, so they come from Box metadata, the new Search in drawings, and the BIM model's own properties. DrawingVQA, a July 2026 drawing benchmark, still put the best model well below experienced professionals, so quantities and compliance stay with people. For Safety hints, the labels show per category where the model earns trust, and that's where coverage expands first.

  6. Why it matters for the role

    At Lará, the AI hotel platform I'm prototyping, the staff assistant answers from the hotel's own procedures. I made one call early: allergy alerts never depend on the AI. They reach the restaurant team and manager within seconds, must be acknowledged, and work with AI switched off. It's the same line Dalux drew with Safety hints. At MR Neo, a CRM and business-automation platform, I made the company AI-native in under two months, starting with a knowledge base connected read-only to the codebase and documentation as one source of truth. I also turned four to five hours of my own testing into AI skills that have found 588 bugs and UX issues, and the team keeps improving them. That's what an eval set is: judgement written down so it can be rerun. Before that, I grew GMNG to 1M+ users, and at XY ADS I architected an ad exchange for 100M+ monthly impressions and directed the developers to cut latency under heavy traffic. That trade-off between speed and cost comes with every AI feature.
