Hi GetYourGuide team, I've built you a stop in my city. I started where a traveller starts: your city pages. I know those pages may sit with another team, but the cards and filters on them are where Discovery comes in.

  1. The flow I looked at

    Your city landing pages: Berlin first, then eleven more on 24 October, on desktop and on a phone, logged out; Berlin, London and Barcelona rechecked on 25 October. Category chips, the "Top activities" carousel, then "All activities". Barcelona has no carousel at all.

  2. What I noticed

    A first-time visitor to Berlin has to choose between many very similar options. The carousel's 12 cards are exactly the first 12 of "All activities", in the same order, and several return under "Our most recommended things to do" and in the FAQ. Nine of the first 18 results are boat cruises, eight of them on the Spree, from at least five different operators. Your page says ranking weighs popularity, ratings, performance, availability, diversity and revenue, including supplier commissions, and that suppliers can raise commissions as marketing. So this is likely the marketplace working as designed, and London shows the carousel can differ from the list (it adds four airport lounges the list doesn't show), so Berlin's match is a choice. My question isn't whether that's a bug. It's whether the page helps a traveller choose between eight similar Spree cruises, while keeping the visibility operators invest in through commissions.

  3. What I'd check, then test

    First I'd check where the travellers who book actually find their activity (carousel, list position, chip or search), split by device and by whether they came from a search engine, and how often people open several similar offers before booking. If they rarely compare, I'd stop there. If they do, I'd test one change: similar offers shown as one entry led by the highest-ranked option ("Spree cruises · compare 8"), so the ranking, commissions included, decides who leads. I'd agree that rule with Commercial before testing, and track impressions and bookings per operator so no one is quietly squeezed out. Success means bookings per visitor and revenue per visitor both hold or rise, with take rate, page speed and search traffic as guardrails. Anything that changes the list search engines read runs as a page-level test on mid-size cities, not on Berlin or London. Next on the agenda: does it hold for small cities and for visitors arriving from search?

  4. Where it could go in 12 months

    Over a year I'd propose one shared way to group and rank offers across every entry point: the carousel, the list, category chips, inspiration modules and, agreed with the landing-page and SEO owners, the templates search engines index. Each entry point gets a distinct job (one inspires, one helps compare), and a traveller facing many similar options sees a clear choice instead of a long scroll. That needs Search and Recommendations for ranking, Brand and Content for how groups are named and written, and Commercial for how commissions work inside a group.

  5. Why it matters for the role

    At GMNG I talked to players before redesigning the homepage and first-time experience (+20% satisfaction), and diagnosed loss aversion behind new-user churn, which led to Everybody Wins (+30% new-user retention). At XY ADS I built a self-serve ad exchange from scratch, integrating many DSPs and SSPs, with $100k a month in ad spend running through it, so I'd bring Commercial in early: grouping changes which operators get seen. At MR Neo I made the company AI-native in under two months, then turned my testing method into AI skills the whole team uses.
