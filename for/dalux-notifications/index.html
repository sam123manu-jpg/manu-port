Hi Dalux team, I've built you a stop in my city. Your ad asks how users can know what needs their attention without being overwhelmed. I tested it on a free Field Basic project with two accounts. A task assigned before the subcontractor accepted the invite sent them no task email, then or when they joined, although their task emails were on: just a red badge inside Dalux. A second task, sent after they joined, arrived twice in the same minute, as a push and an email. When they reported the first one ready, the construction manager also got only a badge. Your public documentation shows the wider pattern: the rules change with the module an event comes from. Below is what I tested, the map, what I'd check first and where it could go. I'm open to relocating to Copenhagen and available from mid-December.

  1. What I tested and read

    On 24 and 25 October 2026 I set up a free Field Basic project with two accounts: a construction manager on the web, and a subcontractor on the web and the iPhone app. I assigned one task before the subcontractor accepted the invite and one after, and had the first reported ready. I also searched all 336 English articles in your help centre, read every one that covers notifications, reminders or inboxes, and went through the Build and FM release notes from 2025 to 2026.6. Field Basic is the free tier, so paid plans may set different defaults, and help articles describe intended behaviour; both are things to check against the product and its data.

  2. What I noticed

    The two accounts started with different defaults: task emails were off for the construction manager who created the project and on for the invited subcontractor; push for tasks was on for both. Neither has a setting for reminders. Task 1, assigned before the subcontractor accepted the invite, sent no task email, then or when they joined; their first sign was a red "1" on Tasks. Task 2, sent after they joined, arrived as a push and an email in the same minute, and the push opened the task directly. When the subcontractor reported task 1 ready, the construction manager, with task emails off and no app, again had only the badge. The daily summaries, on by default for both, arrived late the next afternoon, and both inboxes sorted them into Promotions. Inside Dalux the task is clear (who sent it, the deadline, the message); what reaches you outside it depends on when you joined and on defaults you never set. Across products the documentation shows why this is hard to see. Your guides tell people to "start each day by reviewing your inbox", but in Build that means several: tasks, comments, approvals, submittals and safety observations each wait in their own. Push is described for newly assigned tasks and, loosely, "other registration types". In Box an admin can subscribe every new member to every folder by default. File approvals remind a day before the deadline, tenders 24 hours before, and tasks have a manual Remind to one recipient at a time; comments and submittals have Overdue lists but no reminder. Since 2026.3, a safety manager with task emails on gets each public observation by email and again in the summary, and that email has no switch of its own. FM has its own settings page, with a different admin rule. Each choice makes sense inside its module. Together, nobody can see in one place what will reach them and when.

  3. What I'd check, then test

    With engineering, I'd first build an inventory of every event that can notify, across all Dalux products: who receives it and why (responsible, on a comment's Notify list, subscribed to a folder), which channels it uses, and what it asks them to do. Each project already keeps an email log of every notification email it sends and an activity log of user actions, so for each email type I'd join "sent" to what followed: was the item answered, approved or closed before its deadline? Push and the summary would need their own tracking. Alongside that, interviews and site visits by role: a subcontractor on a phone, a construction manager at a desk, a safety manager, a facility manager, asking what they look at first in the morning, and what they've switched off and why. On Autodesk's ideas board one user says their projects turned email off for most members after complaints; that failure mode, muting everything and missing what matters, is the one to guard against. Then I'd test in steps, as split tests where the product allows: work waiting for someone reaches them when they join; one event arrives once, by push or by email, not both in the same minute; every notification says why you got it and what's needed by when; the daily summary leads with what needs you, before what changed; deadline reminders become visible and adjustable in settings, starting with tasks.

  4. The KPIs I'd own

    Action rate per notification type: of everything that asked someone to do something, how much got done before its deadline. Noise: notifications that lead nowhere, and how often people mute a type or switch the summary off. Misses: tasks and approvals that go overdue without the responsible person opening them. Guardrails: overdue tasks overall, and support tickets about missed items. I'd baseline all of them in the first month, set targets with the team from that baseline, and rank every change by which one it moves.

  5. Where it could go in 12 months

    The goal: one place that shows each person what needs them, ranked, and a shared notification capability behind it that Field and Box send through first, then Tender, Handover and FM. Each product publishes events with the same fields (who it's for and why, what changed, what's needed by when, how urgent), and one service decides channel, timing, grouping and deduplication from one preference model, so a rule is written once, not once per module. The principles get agreed with each product's PM: every notification names its reason, its action and its deadline. Three tiers. Must act (a safety issue, an approval due today) reaches the responsible person even if they've muted the module, and tells the sender if it goes overdue, as file flows already do for approvals. Act soon (assigned to you) arrives by push or instant email, as each person prefers. For information (folder changes, things you follow) waits for the summary. I'd reuse what exists first: FM's standardised emails and notification list, and the project email log as the measurement base. I'd defer personalised ranking until the shared event fields and action-rate data exist, then test it against simple rules.

  6. Why it matters for the role

    I've built a must-not-miss path before. In Lará, the AI hotel platform I'm prototyping, guest requests land in the right team's queue and managers see everything, but allergy alerts follow their own path: they reach the restaurant team and manager within seconds, must be acknowledged, and work with AI switched off. At MR Neo, a CRM and automation platform, I made the whole company AI-native in under two months. There I also found that once someone builds their 21st automation flow they can't see which flows fire first, overlap or contradict each other, and designed warnings that flag it while they build: the same kind of problem as notification rules spread across modules. At GMNG I led a team of nine and grew the platform to 1M+ users. Replacing the registration form with a single phone-number field cut sign-up drop-off by about 60%; I ran A/B tests in Firebase and engagement campaigns in CleverTap; and I learned to trust research over my own habits: I launched card payments, talking to customers showed 80% of depositors used Paytm, and we switched.
